A Series Rental LLC is a specialized business structure that allows a real estate investor to establish a single parent company containing multiple internal "child" sub-units, or series, to legally separate individual rental properties. Think of it as an insurance policy umbrella where each rental property sits inside its own independent, walled-off compartment. [1, 2, 3, 4] 

## 1.0 Series Rental LLC

### 1.1 How it Works for Real Estate

* Parent Umbrella: You register one master LLC with the state business filing office.
* Individual Sub-Series: You create internal subdivisions (e.g., "Series A," "Series B") within that master structure.
* Property Segregation: Each individual rental property is deeded to and held by its own unique sub-series.
* Liability Firewall: If a tenant sues over an incident at Property A, the lawsuit is legally confined to "Series A". The assets in Series B, Series C, and the parent company remain shielded from the claim. [2, 3, 5, 6] 

### 1.2 Core Advantages

* Cost Efficiency: You pay only one initial state filing fee instead of paying separate setup fees for multiple traditional LLCs.
* Simplified Taxes: All operations under the umbrella can generally be consolidated into a single federal tax return.
* Infinite Scalability: You can easily add new properties into new sub-series internally without needing to form brand new companies through the state.
* Asset Insulation: It effectively creates the same "firewall" asset protection of multiple LLCs with less state-level paperwork. [2, 7, 8, 9, 10] 

### 1.3 Operational Requirements & Drawbacks

* Rigid Recordkeeping: You must maintain separate accounting ledger logs and individual bank accounts for every single series to preserve the legal liability shield.
* Financing Hurdles: Traditional mortgage lenders frequently struggle to verify individual series entities, making it harder to secure bank loans at the sub-series level.
* Limited State Acceptance: Only about 15 to 20 states—including Texas, Delaware, Nevada, and Utah—fully recognize Series LLCs.
* Legal Ambiguity: Because the structure is relatively modern, it has less historical precedent in court systems compared to standard corporations or traditional LLCs. [3, 5, 7, 11, 12, 13, 14]


## 2. Convert To Series Rental LLC

To convert your current Texas rental LLC into a Series LLC holding your 2 properties, you must modify your state registration, overhaul your internal corporate records, and legally move the real estate titles. [1, 2] 
## Phase 2.1: State & Internal Restructuring

   1. Pass a Corporate Resolution: Draft and sign an internal authorizing resolution among the LLC members approving the conversion into a Series LLC.
   2. File a Certificate of Amendment: File Form 424 (Certificate of Amendment) with the [Texas Secretary of State](https://www.sos.state.tx.us/corp/formationfaqs.shtml). You must pay a $150 filing fee and explicitly include the statutorily required language stating that the LLC is authorized to establish individual series with limited liability.
   3. Amend your Company Agreement: Completely replace or amend your LLC Operating Agreement. It must outline the rules for establishing internal sub-series (e.g., "Series A" and "Series B"), dictate how assets are assigned, and explicitly state the legal liability separation between them. [1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7] 

## Phase 2.2: Establish the Individual Series [7] 

   1. Choose a Naming Convention: Name your child sub-series clearly. A standard approach integrates the property address and the parent company name (e.g., [Your Parent LLC Name], Series 123 Main St).
   2. Decide Between Protected vs. Registered Series:
   * Protected Series: Formed strictly internally via your updated operating agreement. No additional state filing fee is required, but it is highly recommended to file an Assumed Name Certificate (DBA) via Form 503 with the state for each series ($25 fee each) to open bank accounts.
      * Registered Series: Requires filing a Certificate of Registered Series with the state for each sub-unit, which costs an additional $300 fee per series. This makes the sub-series a matter of public record.
   3. Open Separate Bank Accounts: Establish completely independent business bank accounts for each individual series. Co-mingling funds between the parent, Series A, and Series B will legally destroy your liability firewall. [1, 2, 6, 8, 9, 10, 11, 12] 

## Phase 2.3: Property & Asset Transfer

   1. Execute and Record New Deeds: Draft and execute a new Deed (typically a Special Warranty Deed or Quitclaim Deed) for each property. You will transfer Property 1 from the Parent LLC to [Parent LLC], Series A, and Property 2 to [Parent LLC], Series B. Record these deeds with the county clerk where the land is located.
   2. Update Tenant Leases: Draft lease amendments or execute new lease agreements. The tenant's landlord must officially be updated from the main LLC to the specific sub-series that owns that property. All future rent checks must be deposited directly into that specific sub-series bank account.
   3. Update Insurance Policies: Contact your insurance brokers to update your Property Insurance and Title Insurance. The named insured must match the specific sub-series holding the deed to prevent a carrier from denying a claim based on an incorrect entity name. [9, 10, 13, 14] 

## Consider 

* Do the properties currently have active mortgages, or do you own them free and clear?
* Do you prefer to keep the sub-series entirely private (Protected Series), or visible on public state records (Registered Series)?
* What accounting software do you use to manage rental book keeping? [3]
